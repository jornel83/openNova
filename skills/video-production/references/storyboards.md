# Storyboard preparation and continuity

A clip prompt describes format, subject/reference roles, environment, visible
actions, camera behavior, sequential timing, sound and essential constraints.
Use time blocks to express pacing, not as a guarantee of exact frame timing.

For a single shot, avoid combining contradictory camera moves. For a visual
transition, state what fills the frame before the transition and what becomes
visible after it. A matched color dissolve is usually easier to direct than
asking architecture or props to physically deform into other objects.

Keep a continuity record for recurring characters and props: reference image,
costume, shape, camera angle, and each shot's start/end state. When a palette
accumulates pigments, prepare each incremental state and fix pigment positions.
Resolve conflicting source counts explicitly rather than inventing another
color. Separate protagonist identity references from environment references.

First generate a representative difficult shot before applying the pattern to
all remaining shots. If a multi-stage clip fails, consider splitting it into
shorter clips for local editing, within the user's requested scope and cost
authorization. Report what changed and why.

After generation, compare beginning, intermediate action and ending frames
with the shot's requirements. Hands, moving animals, small droplets and gradual
color transitions need particular attention. Keep the original clips when
assembling the film. Existing visual defects survive concatenation.
